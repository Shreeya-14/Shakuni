# Academic References & Foundational Literature

This document lists foundational peer-reviewed literature in cognitive psychology, behavioral economics, argumentation theory, and computational linguistics underpinning the **Shakuni** analytical framework.

---

## 1. Framing Effects & Cognitive Heuristics

1. **Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1981).**  
   *The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice.*  
   *Science*, 211(4481), 453-458.  
   DOI: [10.1126/science.7455683](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683)  
   *Relevance*: Foundational evidence demonstrating how linguistic framing of identical outcomes radically shifts human decision-making and perceived risk.

2. **Kahneman, D. (2011).**  
   *Thinking, Fast and Slow.*  
   Farrar, Straus and Giroux, New York.  
   *Relevance*: Establishes dual-process theory (System 1 intuitive vs. System 2 analytical thinking), highlighting how emotional pressure and urgency bypass analytical scrutiny.

---

## 2. Persuasion, Social Influence & Compliance

3. **Cialdini, R. B. (2001).**  
   *Influence: Science and Practice* (4th Edition).  
   Allyn & Bacon, Boston.  
   *Relevance*: Defines canonical weapons of influence including social proof (bandwagon), authority pretexts, scarcity/urgency appeals, and reciprocation guilt.

4. **Festinger, L. (1957).**  
   *A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance.*  
   Stanford University Press.  
   *Relevance*: Theoretical background on how individuals reconcile contradictory information and how social pressure induces cognitive conformity.

---

## 3. Argumentation Schemes & Informal Fallacies

5. **Walton, D. (1996).**  
   *Argumentation Schemes for Presumptive Reasoning.*  
   Lawrence Erlbaum Associates, Mahwah, NJ.  
   *Relevance*: Formalizes structural criteria for distinguishing legitimate argument schemes from manipulative informal fallacies (e.g. ad populum, false dilemma, appeal to pity).

6. **Wason, P. C. (1960).**  
   *On the Failure to Eliminate Hypotheses in a Conceptual Task.*  
   *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 12(3), 129-140.  
   DOI: [10.1080/17470216008416717](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470216008416717)  
   *Relevance*: Cognitive foundations of confirmation bias and selective evidence presentation.

---

## 4. Computational Discourse & Digital Manipulation

7. **Mathur, A., Acar, G., Friedman, M. J., Lucherini, E., Mayer, J., Chetty, M., & Narayanan, A. (2019).**  
   *Dark Patterns at Scale: Findings from a Crawl of 11K Shopping Websites.*  
   *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 3(CSCW), 1-32.  
   DOI: [10.1145/3359183](https://doi.org/10.1145/3359183)  
   *Relevance*: Empirical taxonomization of deceptive digital interfaces, informing Shakuni's information withholding category.

8. **Thorne, J., Vlachos, A., Christodoulopoulos, C., & Mittal, A. (2018).**  
   *FEVER: a large-scale dataset for Fact Extraction and VERification.*  
   *Proceedings of the 2018 Conference of the North American Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics (NAACL-HLT)*, 809-819.  
   *Relevance*: Highlights current fact-checking benchmarks and informs Shakuni's complementary analysis of framing beyond factual claims.
